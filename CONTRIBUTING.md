## bring your own tiny computer

CircuitPython? **yes.**  
Arduino? **yes.**  
C++? **yes.**  
QMK? **hell yeah.**  
Some board you found at the bottom of a drawer?

**absolutely.**

If it can act like a keyboard and read three switches, you have the important part.

<div align="center">

| <kbd>1</kbd> | <kbd>2</kbd> | <kbd>3</kbd> |
|:---:|:---:|:---:|
| **YES** | **YES, ALWAYS** | **NO** |

</div>

Give it a directory:

```text
firmware/
├── rp2040-zero/
│   ├── README.md
│   └── code.py
│
└── your-board/
    ├── README.md
    └── your-cool-stuff
```

And leave enough breadcrumbs for someone else to build it:

## broke something? found something?

<div align="center">

### fix it · document it · show us

</div>

Bad instruction? **Rewrite it.**

Firmware bug? **Squash it.**

Made a better wiring diagram? **I need it.**

Got ClaudePad running on another OS? **Add it.**

Changed the switches, enclosure, keycaps, wiring, firmware, or somehow gave the thing RGB?

**You better include a picture.**

---

## send it

You don't need an essay.

When you open a PR, give me:

> **WHAT** — what changed?  
> **WHY** — what made you change it?  
> **TESTED** — what hardware did you actually run it on?

Hardware and firmware changes should be tested on the real board when possible.

It doesn't need to be perfect.

That's what the PR is for.
